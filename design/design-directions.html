My read: The one video with the real Ethan in it got about 90× the views of the AI videos. Three videos is too few to call it a trend, but it lines up with the plan: the real kid and the real dad are the draw, and AI is just the tool. The painting kit fits because the AI draws the lines and Ethan paints them by hand. The site should be built around that pairing.
Positioning: "A father–son studio. AI draws the lines, Ethan brings the color, and Dad holds the camera." Every page carries two voices: Ethan (big, playful, first person) and Dad (short notes from the parent running things). That two-voice setup is what makes the site Ethan's own and not a generic kids' channel template.

6. Questions for you before I build

Q1. Which direction (or mix)?
My pick: 1 + the slider from 3.
Q2. What does the kit contain: a printable PDF only, or a physical canvas later?
"Email in 24 hours" points to a digital PDF, so the mockups assume that. A physical version would only change the checkout copy.
Q3. What price should the checkout show?
$9 in the mockups is a placeholder. Whatever we show is the number we "test" before the free reveal.
Q4. How much of you should be on the site: name and photo, or just "Dad"?
The mockups use "Jason, Ethan's dad" in a couple of spots. That's easy to switch to plain "Dad".
Kid privacy: Ethan's face and name are already public on the channel. On the site I'd still leave out his school, neighborhood and age-plus-birthday details, and collect only the buyer's email (no address, no child info). The simulated checkout never shows card fields, so nobody types payment details into a fake form.
